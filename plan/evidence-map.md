| Bassetti-2026 | [[source-pages/bassetti-2026-sleep-disorders-europe-burden]] | burden analysis | Estimates epidemiology and cost burden for five sleep disorders in 47 European countries. | Adult prevalence estimates: OSA 18%, insomnia 10%, RLS 3%, narcolepsy 0.03%, RBD 0.009%. | Sleep-disorder burden should be presented by disease and region, not as one pooled prevalence. | 1.1-P1 | direct |
| van-Straten-2025 | [[source-pages/van-straten-2025-insomnia-prevalence-meta]] | systematic review/meta-analysis | Estimates insomnia disorder prevalence by diagnostic method in general population studies. | Face-to-face DSM interview pooled prevalence is 12.4%; self-report and scale thresholds differ. | Insomnia burden depends on diagnostic route and should not be merged across definitions. | 1.1-P1 | direct |
| Benjafield-2025 | [[source-pages/benjafield-2025-global-insomnia-burden]] | systematic review/population estimate | Estimates global clinically relevant chronic insomnia burden. | Adult prevalence estimate is 16.2%, about 852 million people, based on 18 studies and population data. | Chronic insomnia represents a large burden but this is a population estimate with heterogeneous definitions. | 1.1-P1 | direct |
| Niu-2025 | [[source-pages/niu-2025-china-osa-prevalence]] | systematic review/meta-analysis | Estimates OSA prevalence in China using PSG or validated portable monitoring. | Pooled OSA prevalence at AHI >= 5 is 11.8%. | OSA burden has disease-specific and region-specific estimates that cannot be combined with insomnia figures. | 1.1-P1 | direct |
| Song-2024 | [[source-pages/song-2024-global-rls-prevalence]] | systematic review/modeling | Estimates RLS prevalence in adults aged 20-79 years. | Global RLS prevalence is 7.12%, about 356.07 million people. | RLS is another disease-specific burden estimate with distinct age and diagnostic boundaries. | 1.1-P1 | direct |
| Riemann-2017 | [[source-pages/riemann-2017-european-insomnia-guideline]] | clinical guideline | Recommends CBT-I for adult chronic insomnia. | CBT-I is first-line, strong recommendation, high-quality evidence. | Standard treatment context exists for chronic insomnia; this does not prove closed-loop efficacy. | 1.1-P2 | direct |
| Edinger-2021 | [[source-pages/edinger-2021-aasm-behavioral-insomnia]] | clinical guideline | Recommends behavioral and psychological treatments for adult chronic insomnia. | Multicomponent CBT-I is strongly recommended; single-component treatments are conditional. | Guideline-backed treatment need is specific to adult chronic insomnia. | 1.1-P2 | direct |
| Schutte-Rodin-2008 | [[source-pages/schutte-rodin-2008-aasm-insomnia-guideline]] | clinical guideline | Defines chronic insomnia and treatment goals. | Insomnia involves adequate sleep opportunity plus sleep difficulty and daytime impairment; goals include sleep quality/quantity and daytime impairment. | Sleep intervention need should connect night symptoms with daytime functional consequences. | 1.1-P2 | direct |
| Dudysova-2024 | [[source-pages/dudysova-2024-closed-loop-insomnia-pilot]] | pilot crossover trial | Tests closed-loop auditory stimulation in chronic insomnia. | Immediate slow oscillation increase, but no memory, macrostructure, arousal-count, or subjective sleep-quality change. | Closed-loop intervention is a testable route, not established clinical efficacy. | 1.1-P2 | direct |
